Overview

The Building Had Changed.
The App Hadn’t.

Bank of America’s internal Flagscape app was built for a different era of work. Post-pandemic return-to-office introduced two new critical needs — health screening and seat booking — but the existing information architecture hadn’t been restructured to accommodate them.

Approach

Getting Everyone
in the Same Room

Before any design work started, I facilitated a two-day cross-functional workshop to cut through the competing priorities and establish a shared direction.

Surface Friction

Surface the real friction points from the people experiencing them — not from stakeholder assumptions about what users needed.

Align Stakeholders

Align stakeholders across functions on what mattered most — creating a prioritization framework the team could defend, not just present.

Evidence Over Opinion

An impact and effort matrix evaluated ideas objectively, moving the conversation away from opinion and toward measurable value.

The workshop used an impact and effort matrix to evaluate ideas objectively. It moved the conversation away from opinion and toward evidence — which features would deliver the most value to employees, and which were expensive distractions.

Solution

Compliance Without
the Friction

The health screening flow had to do two things that usually work against each other: be fast enough that employees would actually use it every day, and rigorous enough to satisfy compliance requirements at scale.

Health Questions

A series of health-related questions determining entry eligibility — structured for speed, not friction.

Automatic Approval

When criteria were met — no extra steps, no confirmation screens. The fastest possible path through compliance.

Automatic Denial

When criteria were not met — clear, immediate, no ambiguity. Defined next steps ensured no dead ends.

The flow was designed to be adaptable as compliance requirements evolved — which, in a post-pandemic environment, they did frequently. Scalability wasn’t an afterthought. It was built into the architecture from the start.

Reflection

What Made
It Work

The real design work happened in the workshop, not in Figma. Getting cross-functional stakeholders aligned on a single prioritized direction — before a single screen was designed — was what made the downstream execution clean. When everyone agrees on what matters and why, design decisions stop being debates.

The Flagscape redesign didn’t just solve a return-to-office problem. It demonstrated what happens when research, strategy, and facilitation come before the interface.

Unified IA

Health screening and seat booking integrated into the existing app architecture without requiring a full rebuild.

Frictionless Compliance

A validation flow rigorous enough for compliance, fast enough for daily use by tens of thousands of employees.

Stakeholder Alignment

Cross-functional agreement on priorities before design began — a framework the team could defend at every decision point.

Adaptable Architecture

A screening flow built to evolve with changing compliance requirements without requiring a redesign each time.
