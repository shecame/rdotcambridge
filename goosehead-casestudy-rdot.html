Overview

A Business Model Transformation
the Website Hadn’t Caught Up To

Insurance is a category built on complexity. Policies, pricing structures, and coverage models create decision fatigue before a customer even gets to a quote. Most insurance websites make this worse, burying the value proposition under jargon and forcing users to work for information they should be handed immediately.

01
The Business Was Evolving

Goosehead was rethinking how insurance should work for clients, aiming for a clearer, more transparent experience, giving clients better visibility into services, pricing, and value.

02
The Website Wasn’t Keeping Up

There was a gap between what the business was becoming and what the website was communicating. The digital experience wasn’t reflecting the shift in the model.

Approach

Structure
Before Surface

The opportunity was clarity, not simplification for its own sake, but precision about what Goosehead actually does and why it’s different. I partnered with stakeholders, developers, and customers to align on three things before design began.

Design

Decisions Grounded
in Research

Insurance sites tend to over explain. I made the call to cut: less copy, fewer features, clearer choices.

Value Proposition First

Clarified the service model so the value proposition was legible within the first scroll, no hunting required.

Transparency by Structure

Pricing and coverage logic surfaced early and built into the architecture.

Progressive Disclosure

Guided decision-making by revealing information at the right moment, rather than presenting everything at once and creating overwhelm.

Getting a Quote

One question per step. Each answer collapses into a summary, so customers can see what they’ve already given and only what comes next.

What I Learned

Test the Hardest
Idea First

Pricing was the hardest part of Goosehead’s model to explain, and it was the last part we settled. It changed more late in the project than anything else, and each change reached back into the structure around it. Next time I would put the pricing explanation in front of customers in the first round of testing, before the architecture was locked.
